Read the units before the number

A normalized training loss is not automatically an angle in radians, a distance in millimetres, or a success probability. In this project, inspect the training configuration and loss weights, then use the evaluator's raw joint-angle errors for an interpretable action comparison. Do not compare two loss numbers if their weighting or normalization changed.

Averages can hide the important instant

Illustrative example: nine joint targets have 0.001 rad error, but one has 0.100 rad error. The mean is 0.0109 rad. That average hides the much larger error at one instant. If that instant is when a gripper releases an object, its timing matters. This example is invented; it is not a result from our robot.

“Two actions per forecast” means executing the first two predictions from a ten-action sequence, checking each before applying it. It does not mean skipping observations or disabling contact checks. Our two-action experiment improved approach progress but did not establish grasping.

Check your interpretation: do valid, low-error predictions prove the robot can grasp?

No. They describe predictions on recorded inputs. Once the policy moves the arms, its new observations can differ from the teacher's. The physical run must independently demonstrate a grasp, transfer and stable release. Our failed runs are evidence of that gap.
